## PART 1: WEEKLY LESSON PLAN & MATERIALS
*Please generate the following content, organized by session.*

### SESSION 1: EXPLORE & DO (Approx. 30-40 min)

**A. Child-Facing Materials for Session 1**
1. **Title:** A catchy, kid-friendly title for the week's lesson.
2. **Kick-Off Hook:** A single, boldfaced question to frame the concept.
3. **Philosophical Explainer (Part 1):**
   * An overview paragraph (4-5 sentences) stating the core idea.
   * The first 1-2 numbered sections of the explainer. Each section should have a simple noun-phrase heading and be 3-4 sentences long.
   * Define tougher words in-line. Example: "...your autonomy (the right to make your own choices)."
   * Include one "What Do You Think?" dilemma box within this section.

**B. Teacher Guide for Session 1**
1. **Objective:** What the child will be introduced to in this session.
2. **Step-by-Step Plan:**
   * **Hook (5 min):** How to use the Kick-Off question to spark curiosity.
   * **Read & Discuss (10-15 min):** Instructions for reading the Explainer text and discussing the "What Do You Think?" box.
   * **Hands-On Activity (15-20 min):** A simple, creative, hands-on activity that physically demonstrates the lesson's theme. Include a "You Will Need" list and step-by-step instructions. (If I provided an activity idea, build on that. Otherwise, create a new one.)

---

### SESSION 2: CREATE & PRACTICE (Approx. 30-40 min)

**A. Child-Facing Materials for Session 2**
1. **Philosophical Explainer (Part 2):**
   * The remaining 2-3 numbered sections of the explainer.
   * Include a final "What Do You Think?" dilemma box.
2. **Vocabulary Table:**
   * A two-column table with "New Word" and "What It Means."
   * Include 5-7 key vocabulary words from the entire explainer.
   * Definitions must be kid-level and easy to understand.
3. **Creative Corner: Draw Your Idea:**
   * Describe a simple, line-art style image representing the core concept.
   * Provide a short prompt inviting the child to draw it. Example: "Draw a picture of two friends building a tower together. How can you show they are being good friends in your drawing?"
4. **Printable Worksheet: Vocabulary Practice:**
   * Create a one-page worksheet. For each vocabulary word from the table, include:
      * The word in a large, traceable (dotted-line) font.
      * A blank, lined space for freehand practice.
      * A simple sentence using the word, with a lined space below for the child to copy the sentence.

**B. Teacher Guide for Session 2**
1. **Objective:** What the child will reinforce and practice in this session.
2. **Step-by-Step Plan:**
   * **Recap (5 min):** A quick question or game to review Session 1's main idea.
   * **Read & Create (15 min):** Instructions for reading the rest of the explainer and then moving to the "Creative Corner" drawing activity.
   * **Vocabulary & Writing Practice (15-20 min):** How to introduce the Vocabulary Table and guide the child through the Printable Worksheet.

---

### SESSION 3: REFLECT & CONNECT (Optional, Approx. 20-25 min)

**A. Child-Facing Materials for Session 3**
1. **Exit Exercises:**
   * **Check Your Thinking:** 3 multiple-choice questions checking key vocabulary/ideas from the week.
   * **Talk About It:** 1 open-ended reflection prompt for a parent-child discussion. Frame it as "Talk with a grown-up about..." or "Tell a story about..."

**B. Teacher Guide for Session 3**
1. **Objective:** To consolidate learning and connect the philosophical concept to personal experience.
2. **Step-by-Step Plan:**
   * **Warm-Up (5 min):** Ask the child to share their drawing from Session 2 and explain their thinking.
   * **Check for Understanding (10 min):** How to present the "Check Your Thinking" questions in a low-stakes, conversational way.
   * **Deeper Conversation (10 min):** How to use the "Talk About It" prompt to facilitate a meaningful wrap-up discussion.

## PART 2: TEACHER RESOURCE KIT (For the Whole Week)
*Please clearly mark this section as "FOR TEACHER/PARENT EYES ONLY."*

1. **Deeper Learning Notes:**
   * For each numbered section heading and each dilemma box from the child's materials, provide:
      * **The Big Idea:** The core philosophical concept a child might discover.
      * **Likely Student Responses:** 2–3 potential answers or thoughts the child might have.
      * **Expert Clarification:** A concise, simple explanation the adult can use to guide the conversation.
2. **Answer Keys:**
   * Provide clear answers for the 3 multiple-choice "Check Your Thinking" questions.
   * Provide guidance and potential discussion points for the open-ended "Talk About It" prompt.
3. **Teacher Backgrounder:**
   * A half-page (≈200-250 words) scholarly summary of how philosophers traditionally approach the topic. This should be written for an educated adult, providing context and key philosophical viewpoints (e.g., mentioning Aristotle on friendship, Rawls on fairness, or Kant on rules).

## PART 3: BRINGING PHILOSOPHY TO LIFE (Interactive Add-ons)
*Based on the week's theme (e.g., "What is Real?", "What is Fair?"), generate the following three interactive prompts:*

1. **Interactive Thought Experiment:**
   * Create a short, child-centric "What if...?" scenario or dilemma that puts her directly inside the philosophical problem. Frame it as a puzzle with no right answer.
   * *Example for "Identity":* "What if you went to sleep tonight, and tomorrow you woke up in your friend's body, and your friend woke up in your body? You have your memories and thoughts, but everyone sees you as your friend. How would you convince me that you are still you?"
2. **Thinking Journal Prompt:**
   * Provide one evocative, open-ended question that she can respond to by drawing, doodling, or writing a few words in her journal. It should connect the big idea to her personal world.
   * *Example for "Fairness":* "Draw a picture of a time you felt something was unfair. What did 'unfair' look like? What did it feel like in your body?"
3. **Philosophy in Play:**
   * Suggest one tangible, play-based activity using common items (LEGOs, Magnatiles, Play-Doh, drawings, simple movements) that creates a physical metaphor for the week's abstract concept.
   * *Example for "Causality":* "Let's build a 'Cause & Effect' machine with dominoes or books. We'll set them up in a line. What is the 'cause' of the last domino falling? Let's see how changing one small thing at the beginning (the 'cause') creates a totally different result (the 'effect')."
